- 2026-11-23: stages 8 and 9 built (D-035, D-036; 159 tests pass) and run on the two pilots as two runs (pilot 1: analyst v1, articles whole; pilot 2: analyst v2, long articles in parts; both reviewed with the amended reviewer v2). Compared on the 23 articles in both. Aggregate statistics are stable: all 20 shares with enough statements differ between the runs by less than the uncertainty from the sample of articles (largest gap 5 points, e.g. aesthetic value 74% vs 79%, against ±10); positive 73% vs 74%, literature 60% vs 63%, quoted 11% vs 11%, reviewer deleted 9.3% vs 9.1%; kept statements per article correlate 0.96 between the runs. At this sample size (23 articles) the choice of articles matters far more than the run. Individual judgements are much less stable: of 293 distinct judgements only 209 (71%) were found by both runs; 19% only by pilot 2, which found 11% more statements, mostly in the articles it split (69% shared there, 77% in articles sent whole). On paired judgements the runs agree on art (100%), polarity (99%), quotation (100%), criterion 90% (kappa 0.74; disagreements mostly aesthetic value vs usefulness or tradition), evidence level 73% (kappa 0.47) and the reviewer's deletion 93% (kappa 0.44). Rare values (music, moral value, novelty, sarcasm, `other`, `suggested`) have fewer than 10 statements and cannot be judged on the pilot. Consequence for use of the data: counts and shares over many statements can be reported from one run; claims about single passages, and any statistic on rare values, need several runs or a warning.
